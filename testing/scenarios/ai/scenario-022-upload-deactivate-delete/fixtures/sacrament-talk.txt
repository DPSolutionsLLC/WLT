The Weight of Small Covenants
Brother Daniel Okonjo, sacrament meeting, 8 March 2026

Brothers and sisters, I have been asked to speak about covenants, and I
want to begin somewhere unglamorous: with the ones that are small.

We tend to think of covenant as a word reserved for the temple, or for
baptism, or for a moment large enough that we remember where we were
standing. But the sacrament we took a few minutes ago is a covenant, and
we take it fifty-two times a year, and most of those times we will not
remember afterwards. That is not a failure of devotion. It is the shape
of the thing. A covenant renewed weekly is not meant to be a weekly
crisis. It is meant to become a habit of turning back.

I served for three years as a home teacher to a widower named Brother
Whitfield, who had stopped coming to church long before I was assigned
to him. For most of those three years, nothing happened. I visited, we
talked about his garden, and I left. I reported that nothing had changed,
because nothing had.

In the third year he asked me, without any preamble, whether I thought
the Lord kept track of the years a person spends away. I told him I
thought the Lord kept track of the years a person spends coming back. He
came back the following month. He has not missed a Sunday since.

I tell that story not because it is dramatic but because it is not. The
work that mattered was the two years of nothing. The conversation was
only possible because there had been two years of showing up with no
result to show for it.

The Savior's covenant with us has that same quality. It does not depend
on our having something to report. It asks that we turn back, and it
asks it again next week, and the asking is itself the mercy.

May we be patient with the small covenants, and may we be patient with
each other while they do their slow work. In the name of Jesus Christ,
amen.
