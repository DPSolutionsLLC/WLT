Brothers and sisters, I want to speak this morning about faith, and about how
much of it turns out to be ordinary.

We tend to picture faith at its most dramatic: the moment of rescue, the
answer that arrives exactly when it was needed, the account that ends well
enough to be told from a pulpit. Those moments are real. They are also rare,
and building a life on the expectation of them is building on weather.

Most faith is quieter than that. It is the decision to keep a commitment made
on a better day. It is going to a home you have already visited twice with
nothing obvious to show for it. It is teaching a class you are not sure anyone
listened to, and preparing it again the following week anyway.

I have watched members of this Church carry burdens for years without ever
receiving the explanation they were owed. They kept covenants they did not
feel. They served people who did not thank them. And when I have asked them
why, almost none of them said anything about certainty. They said something
closer to: this is who I decided to be.

That is faith. Not the absence of doubt, but the presence of a decision that
doubt was not permitted to overturn.

To those who feel their faith is small, remember that a seed is small too, and
nobody faults a seed for being one. What is asked of a seed is not that it be
impressive but that it be planted, and then that it be left in the ground long
enough for something to happen that could not have happened in a week.

Hold on to what you do know while you wait for what you do not. And be patient
with the season you are in, for the ordinary work of faith is still the work,
and heaven has never once mistaken it for anything less.
